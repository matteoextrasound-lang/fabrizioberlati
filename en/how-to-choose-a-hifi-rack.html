How to choose a hi-fi rack Measurements, room for air and cables, where to put the turntable and the records: what to check before you buy

You choose a hi-fi rack with a tape measure in hand, not just by looking at photos. A few checks are enough to avoid the shelf that's too low, the turntable that picks up vibration or the amplifier that overheats.

By Fabrizio Berlati · Artisan workshop in Cesena

Start from the equipment you own.

Before looking at racks, list what will go on it: amplifier, CD player, streamer, turntable, maybe a phono preamp or a DAC. Think too about what you might add in a year or two. A spare shelf costs little today; finding out tomorrow that there's no room costs a new rack.

That list alone tells you how many shelves you need.

Measure everything, with a tape measure.

For each unit you need three measurements: width, depth and height. Many hi-fi components are about 43–44 cm wide, but there are narrower models too, and turntables are a story of their own.

Then add to the unit's measurements the space it needs to work. Behind, you need a few centimetres for plugs and cables, more if the cables are thick or stiff. Above, especially for the amplifier, it needs air: manufacturers such as Rega and Rotel specify at least 10 cm of free space around the unit. I go into it in the guide on where to put your amplifier.

This is where most people go wrong: the shelf looks tall enough, then the amplifier only just fits and runs hot.

The turntable goes on top.

The top shelf is the handiest to use and also the steadiest, because it's furthest from the floor. That's where the turntable belongs. Never put it on top of the amplifier: it blocks the vents the heat escapes from, and the transformer can make the cartridge hum.

And keep the speakers on their own supports, not on the same rack as the turntable. You'll find why in the guide on how to isolate a turntable from vibration.

It has to be steady, and level.

A hi-fi rack shouldn't wobble when you touch it, and the shelves shouldn't sag under the weight. Check how much your equipment weighs: a big amplifier can top 20 kg, and on a thin shelf it shows. Solid wood with thick shelves carries it easily and damps vibrations instead of amplifying them.

Once it's home, level the rack, especially if the turntable goes on it. If the floor isn't flat, adjustable feet or a shim under one corner will do.

Open or with doors?

An open rack lets air circulate and lets the remote control signal through. With closed doors the heat stays inside, and with solid doors the remote can't reach the equipment. If you really want doors, choose glass and leave the back open.

For a hi-fi system, open is usually better.

Cables.

More cables run behind a rack than you'd think. An open back, or at least one with cable openings, saves you taking everything apart each time you add a component. If you can, keep power cables apart from signal cables.

Leave room to reach behind with your hand too: sooner or later a cable will need moving.

What about records?

If you play vinyl, it's worth thinking straight away about where to keep it. An LP sleeve is about 31.5 cm, so a record compartment needs to be at least 33 cm high and deep. Everything you need to know is in the guide on how to store vinyl records.

A rack that holds turntable, electronics and records together saves space and keeps things tidy. That's how I designed the Vinyl.

And in the end, looks matter too.

The rack lives in your living room and you see it every day, often more than the equipment on it. Look at the wood, the colour, the proportions, and how it sits with the rest of the room.

Frequently asked questions.

How much space should you leave above the amplifier?

Manufacturers such as Rega and Rotel specify at least 10 cm of free space around the unit. Nothing should sit on top of the amplifier, not even the turntable.

Which shelf should the turntable go on?

The top one: it's the steadiest, the furthest from the floor and the handiest. Never on top of the amplifier.

How deep should a hi-fi rack be?

As deep as your deepest unit, plus a few centimetres behind for plugs and cables. Measure your equipment first, then choose the rack.

Is an open rack or one with doors better?

For hi-fi, usually open: air circulates and the remote works. With doors the heat stays inside.

How tall should a record compartment be?

At least 33 cm, because an LP sleeve is about 31.5 cm. A few extra centimetres make them easier to pull out.
